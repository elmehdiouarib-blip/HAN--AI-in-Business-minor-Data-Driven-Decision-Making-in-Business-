## Pitch: one minute, the five card questions

1. **What does the AI do?** It gives a delivery date at the quote, and it learns roast curves from machines in the field.
2. **Whose AI is it?** Interpretation: in our picture, a Chinese national industrial model. China's 2026 manufacturing plan aims for three to five general-purpose large models in manufacturing, 1,000 industrial AI agents and 100 industrial datasets by 2027 (MIIT, translated by CSET). The data runs on Chinese clouds.
3. **Who gains, and who pays?** The owner gains reliable dates, and customers get honest promises. The planner's job changes from chasing parts to deciding. The firm pays with its data, which also feeds the national model.
4. **What does it need from the country?** A government that steers. The State Council's "AI+" plan says "by 2035, China will have comprehensively created an AI-integrated economy and society" (United States Studies Centre, Jan 2026). It also needs shared datasets, cheap energy and factory talent.
5. **What economy is China building?** Interpretation: a factory-first, state-architected AI economy. The models are shared and the data is pooled, and the government sets the targets.

**For the wall discussion: and Europe?** Interpretation: a Dutch builder would own its data, and the AI Act would protect it. But it would have to assemble the tools itself, and our evidence says most firms do not yet know their own data (handbook page 1). Is that "behind", or a route of our own?
