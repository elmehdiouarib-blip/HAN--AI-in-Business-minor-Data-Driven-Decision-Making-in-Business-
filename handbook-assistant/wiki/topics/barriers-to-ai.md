# Barriers to AI

**Sub-questions:** E3 · **Last updated:** 10 Oct 2026, by /wiki

## What we know
### Accepted
- Among Dutch firms that considered AI but do not use it, 73% name lack of experience as the main reason (2025). [C002, S002]
- 49% of them name privacy as a reason. [C003, S002]
- 42% name unclear legal consequences, such as liability for damage. [C004, S002]
- Three quarters of Dutch firms neither use AI nor have considered it (2025). [C005, S002]
- Among EU firms that considered AI but did not use it, the most common reasons were lack of relevant expertise (70.89%), unclear legal consequences (52.52%) and privacy concerns (48.83%). [C012, S003]
- In the OECD's 2026 survey of over 2,000 SMEs, time constraints, maintenance costs and skills gaps continued to hinder effective implementation. [C024, S006]

### Unchecked (not evidence yet)
- Only 15% of Dutch firms that considered AI say they do not find it useful. [S002, unchecked]
- Among EU firms that considered AI, 20.68% found it not useful, the least common reason. [S003, unchecked]

## How it fits together
Interpretation: the barriers are about knowing how, not about wanting to: few firms say AI is not useful. [C002, C012, S002 unchecked]

## Debates
- **Legal worry, Netherlands against the EU.** 42% of Dutch firms that considered AI name unclear legal consequences [C004, S002] · 52.52% of EU firms do [C012, S003]. The groups differ (Dutch firms with 2+ staff; EU firms with 10+ staff), so this is a difference, not a contradiction.

## Open gaps
- Unknown: barriers reported by Dutch machine builders specifically.
- Unknown: what the partner firm itself sees as its barrier (needs field research).
