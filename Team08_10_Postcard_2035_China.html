Riverbend Roasters · workshop note Wuhan, Tuesday 14 May 2035

Dear Sanne,

You asked what AI changed for us. Here is my Tuesday.

At nine a café chain in Chengdu asked for 40 roasters. Our planning agent read the order book, the supplier feeds and the welding queue, and proposed a delivery date in about a minute.1 It flagged one risk: a burner part from a late supplier. I moved the date a week, checked it with the workshop and pressed send. The date is mine. The agent only advises.

The agent runs on a national industrial model, and our order data helps train it.2 The roasters we ship learn roast curves from the cafés that use them.3 Customers trust our dates now, and they call us less.

My job used to be chasing parts. Now I decide which promises we can keep.

Still hard
The model knows our factory better than we do, and nobody can tell me who owns what it learned from us.

WeiPlanner, Riverbend Roasters (120 people)

Sanne Bakker
Neude 7
Utrecht, the Netherlands

Where this comes from

  1. Our use case from handbook page 2. In two German build-to-order firms, an early date estimate lowered the risk of late delivery but had 35% more error than the planners, so a person decides. Rokoss et al., 2024.
  2. China's 2026 AI + Manufacturing plan (MIIT and seven other bodies) aims for 3 to 5 general-purpose large models in manufacturing, 1,000 industrial AI agents and 100 industrial datasets by 2027 (CSET translation). The State Council's AI+ plan aims for an AI-integrated economy and society by 2035 (USSC, 12 Jan 2026).
  3. Front-runner: Kaleido, a coffee-roaster maker in Wuhan, says its roasters learn custom roast curves with AI assistance. That is the vendor's own story: it shows the product exists, not that it works (Kaleido, 17 Jun 2025).

Pitch

Our pitch in one minute

Team 08 · region China · sector: Dutch-style builders of food-processing machines, here a coffee-roaster maker

1What does the AI do?
Gives a realistic delivery date at the quote, and learns roast curves from machines in use.
2Whose AI is it?
A Chinese national industrial model, trained on pooled factory data on Chinese clouds.
3Who gains, who pays?
The owner gets reliable dates and customers get honest promises. The planner moves from chasing parts to deciding. The firm pays with its data.
4What does it need from the country?
A government that steers (targets to 2027 and 2035), shared datasets, cheap energy and factory talent.
5What economy is China building?
Factory-first and state-architected: shared models, pooled data, targets set from the top.

And Europe? A Dutch builder keeps its own data and has the AI Act's protection. But it has to assemble the tools itself, and our handbook page 1 shows most firms do not yet know their own data. Behind, or a route of our own?
